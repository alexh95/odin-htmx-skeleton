# M1 audit: the request-path substrate and the fork

An independent audit of milestone M1 (PLAN §19.2), by an agent that did not write the code. It applies the
review rubric (§19.2.2), the severity scale (§19.2.3) and the crash-zone rules of D15, and reads the vendored
odin-http diff line by line (D11).

**Method.** Read-only. A parity-gate measurement was running on the same machine, so nothing was built, checked,
tested or run: no `odin build/check/test`, no server, no k6, no Playwright, no drills. Every finding comes from
reading the source at the commit below; where reading could not settle a point, the finding says "suspected" and
names what would confirm it. Line numbers are at `bf831da`.

**Result.** 1 CRITICAL, 0 HIGH, 6 MEDIUM, 9 LOW. M1 cannot exit until M1-01 is fixed (§19.2.1: exit needs 0 open
CRITICAL or HIGH).

**Resolution** (added by the session that wrote the code, after reading each finding against the source).
- **M1-01, fixed** by the fork's F-14 (`body.odin`; `UPSTREAM.md`): a missing CRLF after chunk data is a 400,
  trailers are discarded under a 32-line cap, chunk sizes are 1–15 plain hex digits. Reproduced first on the
  pre-fix build: 101 such requests answered 500 and the 102nd found the process gone (`why="too many recoveries"`,
  exit 70). On the fixed build each payload, 150 times, answers 400 or the route's own status with `/livez` 200
  throughout; the drills' malformed set carries the payloads now, and passes on both profiles.
- **M1-06, fixed**: `writer_run` fails the batch when `guard.call` reports a crash, so its ops answer 500.
- **M1-08, fixed**: `app.serve` installs `obs.logger()` before the server's threads start; the fork's
  `connection … already closing` line is debug now.
- **The rest are open**, each in TODO with its owner: the MEDIUM findings with M2, which reworks the drain and the
  deferred paths for streams; the LOW ones with M2 or later. 0 CRITICAL and 0 HIGH are open.
- **M2's resolutions** (M2 slice 14, by the session that wrote the code):
  - **M1-03, fixed**: the exits arm themselves before they log. A dirty crash writes the crash file and starts its
    5.5 s hard exit first; the watchdog's hook writes the crash file and arms an exit 1 s out, then the watchdog logs
    and exits 70 itself; and every write on those paths waits for the log lock at most 250 ms, then drops
    (`obs.bound_writes`). The drills' `logstall` phase stops reading the server's stderr and stdout until the loops
    block: the process hung for good before the fix; now the watchdog exits 70 about 8 s after the stall, with its
    crash file.
  - **M1-07, fixed**: each loop opens its reader as it starts (`store.on_loop_start`, which the SQLite store
    registers at open and `platform/app` runs from F-4's hook), so a descriptor short fails at boot; a reader that
    still can't open later (a query-pool worker's, or after a reopen) makes `read` return nil, which SQLite's binds
    and reset take as a no-op and `next_row` reports as `.Failed`: a 500, and serving goes on. The store's test
    points a fresh thread's reader at a path that can't open: the read fails, where the process used to exit.
  - **M1-04, fixed**: `web.resume` gives the guard an unwind for the call it runs in (`guard.set_unwind`;
    `web.finish` clears it). A crash between the two runs it after the crash line, which now names the request,
    and the fork's new `response_crashed` answers 500 and closes, as `connection_crashed` does on its own paths. The
    writer's replies and the pools' `on_done` need no change: the unwind travels with the thread, not the caller,
    since `store` and `workers` (L2) can't import `web`. The drills' `continuation` kind crashes in a continuation
    after resume, as those two run one: 500 (the client timed out before the fix), and one crash line naming the
    route.
  - **M1-02, fixed**: the fork's `send_timeout` (`SEND_TIMEOUT_MS`, 30 s) is a deadline on every send a response or
    a stream posts; the sweep cuts a connection still sending past it (`shutdown`, as F-9's drain does), and the
    pending send's failure closes it. On Windows the cut needed F-19 first: core:nbio left accepted sockets without
    the listener's context, so `shutdown` failed there, which had also made F-9's own cut a no-op. The drills'
    `slowread` phase pipelines 100 requests for og.png (23 MB) and reads nothing: cut at the deadline, 1 answer in.
  - **M1-26, fixed** with F-18: a crash in a callback of a stream that has already handed its connection back is
    that connection's (`connection_crashed`: 500, close, the scan trampoline cleared), not the stream's. The drills'
    `streamcrash` phase ends a stream and sends a crashing request on the same socket at once: 500, the connection
    closed, serving on.
- **The re-read's findings** (M1-17 to M1-23, the Re-read section below), resolved by the same session:
  - **M1-17, confirmed CRITICAL and fixed** by the fork's F-16. On the build the parity gate measured (`48e89e5`, Windows,
    `-o:speed`), 12,000 one-byte chunks to `POST /contacts` and 200,000 to `GET /healthz` each overflowed a loop's

    `scanner_scan` is a trampoline now: a scan asked for from inside a scan callback is queued and run by the outermost
    call once the callback returns, so the depth is constant whatever the buffer holds. On the fixed build the same
    12,000 chunks parse and reach the handler (422 for the junk form), and the larger bodies answer 413 at the framing
    cap; the drills' malformed set carries both.
  - **M1-18, fixed**: chunk framing (size lines, line ends, trailers) counts against the body cap, with at least 4 KiB,
    and chunk-size lines and line ends are scanned with a 4 KiB `max_token_size`. A request's buffer is bounded by
    about twice its cap. Compaction within a request stays upstream's TODO; with the bound it is no longer needed.
  - **M1-19, fixed**: a scan error in the trailers is answered as an error.
  - **M1-20, fixed**: chunk framing takes CRLF only (`scan_crlf_lines`; trailers, which are field lines, keep the bare-LF
    tolerance RFC 9112 2.2 allows), whitespace only before a `;`, and leading zeros don't count toward 15 digits.
  - **M1-21, fixed**: `#assert(size_of(int) == 8)` beside the bound.
  - **M1-22, fixed**: the fork's client-caused parse and send failures log at info; `obs.str` writes the other C0
    bytes and DEL as `\xNN`.
  - **M1-23, fixed**: `app/vendor/odin-http/body_test.odin` tests the size parse and the CRLF split (the fork now runs
    in `unit-tests.sh`, its upstream test's leak fixed); the router test looks up under the runner's allocator and pins
    mount order and the two-segment bucket.
  - F-16 was re-read at `8c4f1a9` (the Re-read of F-16 section): 0 CRITICAL, 0 HIGH, 0 MEDIUM, and six LOW findings.
- **The F-16 re-read's findings** (M1-24 to M1-29), resolved by the same session:
  - **M1-24, fixed**: the line bound is in the splits, where it holds whatever the buffer's size: `scan_crlf_lines`
    answers `.Too_Long` (413) past 4 KiB without a line end, and trailers go through `scan_trailer_lines`, which does

  - **M1-25, fixed**: `response_defer` starts its watch only when no read is in flight or queued
    (`next_callback == nil`).
  - **M1-26, open, owner M2** (with M1-04): no M1 profile streams. Fixed in M2 (M2's resolutions, above).
  - **M1-27, fixed by logging**: a drain that fails after the handler answered logs the refusal and the status it

  - **M1-28, fixed**: `CMD=test build.sh`/`build.bat` pass `-define:ODIN_TEST_FAIL_ON_BAD_MEMORY=true`; both profiles'

### M1-01 · CRITICAL · a chunked body reaches an assertion

**Where.** The fork's chunked decoder, `_body_chunked` (`app/vendor/odin-http/body.odin:199-330`), is upstream
code that the fork did not touch; F-6 hardened only the Content-Length path.

- `body.odin:267`: `assert(len(token) == 0)` after each chunk's data. A chunk not followed by CRLF
  (`1\r\nab\r\n`) delivers `"b"` there.
- `body.odin:294`: each trailer line goes through `header_parse(&s.req.headers, …)`, which calls
  `headers_entry_unsafe` (`http.odin:190`), which asserts `!h.readonly` (`headers.odin:58`). The request headers
  were made read-only at the end of the header section (`server.odin:820`), before any body is read, so **any**
  trailer field other than a duplicate `Host` (`0\r\nX: y\r\n\r\n`) trips it. Only the success branch
  (`body.odin:285-287`) lifts the flag.
- `body.odin:217`: the chunk size is parsed with `strconv.parse_i64_of_base`, which takes `+` and `_` and wraps
  past 64 bits (`10000000000000005` hex reads as 5). Not a crash; a framing ambiguity with any proxy that forwards
  chunk sizes verbatim.
- Trailers have no count or byte cap: once the assert is fixed, a client can stream trailer lines (up to 64 KiB
  each) into the headers map for the whole body timeout.

**Reach.** Release builds keep assertions (`build.sh` passes no `-disable-assert`; the Dockerfile builds with
`-o:speed`). Two unauthenticated paths reach the decoder:

1. Any write route with a body: `front` → `web.read_body` → `http.body` (`platform/app/front.odin:72`), which
   picks the chunked decoder whenever `Transfer-Encoding` ends in `chunked`. `front` refuses a bad
   Content-Length (`web.content_length_ok`) but not a chunked body.
2. **Any request at all**, GET included: after the handler answers, `response_send` drains a body the handler
   didn't read by calling `body()` itself (`response.odin:313-320`). `GET /healthz` with
   `Transfer-Encoding: chunked` and a trailer reaches the assert after `/healthz` has answered. So does
   `OPTIONS *`, which odin-http answers itself.

**Scenario.** Each request traps inside `guard.invoke` (the scanner's read callback), with no lock held, nothing
posted and no SQLite frame, so the guard calls it pure: the connection closes (with a 500 if nothing was sent) and
serving goes on. But each recovery counts toward `GUARD_BURST` (100 a minute, `guard.odin:138-145`), and the 101st
is reclassified dirty: "too many recoveries", drain, **exit 70**. One client therefore stops the process with about
101 small requests, and keeps it down across Docker's restart backoff (§10.3). D15 rule 6 lists exactly this class
("remotely triggerable crashes are fixed regardless; the guard is defence in depth"), and without the guard it
would be F1 again: one packet, one dead process. Confirmed by reading for a direct connection (the default
loopback bind, `BIND_ALL` on a container, a LAN client). Through the stack's Caddy, malformed framing is rejected
by Go's parser, but a declared trailer (`Trailer: X` plus `X: y`) may be forwarded: suspected, confirm with one
request through the proxy. The fuzz smoke does not cover it: `drills.mjs`'s `MALFORMED` list has two chunked
cases (`-5` and an oversized size) and neither a trailer nor a missing CRLF.

**Fix.** In the fork (a new F-14 row in `UPSTREAM.md`):
- replace the `assert` at `body.odin:267` with `s.cb(s.user_data, "", .Bad_Read_Count)`;
- read trailers without storing them (the app never reads one), or lift `readonly` around `header_parse`; either
  way count trailer bytes against `limit_headers` and fail with `.Too_Long` past it;
- parse the chunk size with a strict hex parser in the style of `parse_content_length` (hex digits only, at most 16,
  overflow-checked; the extension after `;` still dropped).

Add the two payloads (`1\r\nab\r\n0\r\n\r\n` and `0\r\nX: y\r\n\r\n`, on a POST and on a GET) to the `MALFORMED`
list and a unit test in the fork. Optionally, `front` can answer a request whose body it doesn't read with
`connection: close` so `response_send` never drains one; the fork fix is still required for the write routes.

### M1-02 · MEDIUM · no deadline on a response send

**Where.** F-7's sweep only looks at connections with a read in flight
(`server.odin:622`: `if sc.recv_op == nil … { continue }`). While a response is being sent
(`response.odin:340`) the scanner has no read posted (it posts the next one in `clean_request_loop`, after the
send completes), so no timeout of any kind applies.

**Scenario.** A client sends `GET /static/og.png` (230 KB, which has no compressed form) with a small receive
buffer and never reads. The kernel takes what fits; the rest of the send stays posted. The
connection stays `.Active` with no read in flight, so the sweep skips it forever; TCP's persist probes keep a
zero-window peer alive indefinitely. Each such connection holds a file descriptor, a `conn_count` slot (F-8) and
its arena, which holds a full copy of the body (`body_set` copies into `_buf`, `response.odin:41-45`):
10,000 connections pin about 2.3 GB. On Linux the cap (`MAX_CONNS`) then turns every new client away with 503;
on Windows there is no cap. Behind a proxy that forwards a slow download as it is read, the proxy's upstream
connection stalls the same way (suspected for Caddy's defaults; confirm with a slow-read client through the
stack). The drain bounds it (`net.shutdown` past `DRAIN_DEADLINE_MS`), nothing else does.

**Fix.** Give the sweep a send deadline: record one on the connection when `response_send_got_body` (and a
stream's `_stream_kick`) posts a send, for example 30 s or a minimum rate, and past it `net.shutdown(.Both)` the
socket so the pending send fails and the normal close path runs. Add a slow-read client to the shutdown and fd
drills.

### M1-03 · MEDIUM · the exits that end a hang can hang on the log lock

**Where.** Every log line leaves through `write_out` (`obs.odin:105-109`): `sync.mutex_lock(&state.write_mu)` then
a blocking `os.write` to stderr. The watchdog's `_fatal` logs before it exits (`watchdog.odin:79-88`; the
watchdog thread has no buffer, so its line goes straight to `write_out`). A dirty crash's `on_crash`
(`crash.odin:28-43`) calls `guard.log_crash`, which flushes (`guard.odin:224`), **before** it writes the crash file
and arms the 5.5 s hard exit; the hard-exit thread itself logs before `os.exit(70)`.

**Scenario.** Stderr is a pipe whose reader stops: a logging driver in blocking mode that is stuck, a full disk
under one, or `server | tee` with `tee` stopped. The first loop that flushes blocks inside `os.write` holding
`write_mu`; every other loop blocks on `write_mu` at its next flush (within 1 s); `/livez` stops answering.
After 10 s the watchdog fires, and blocks on the same lock in `_fatal`. Nothing exits: no exit 70, no
`last-crash.txt`, and Docker outside Swarm never restarts an unhealthy container (§10.3). D15 rule 4 says the
watchdog "turns every hang into an exit"; this hang it can't. The same order makes a dirty crash during a log
stall hang instead of exiting. (`write_mu` is also a plain `sync.Mutex`, so a trap under it would be classified
pure; see M1-13.)

**Fix.** On the exit paths, arm the exit before logging: in `_fatal` and `on_crash`, write the crash file and
start the hard-exit timer first, then log with `mutex_try_lock` (give up after a few hundred ms) or with one
direct `write` that bypasses `write_mu`. Consider making the loops' flush non-blocking as well (drop and count
lines when the lock is held too long), so a slow log pipe degrades logging rather than serving.

### M1-04 · MEDIUM · a crash in a write continuation leaves the connection open forever

**Where.** A write's continuation (`contacts_web.created`, `updated`, `deleted`, `notes_web.added`,
`gallery_web.submitted`) runs from `on_replies` inside `guard.call` (`writer.odin:219-227`). After a successful
`web.resume` the response belongs to the continuation. If the continuation traps, `guard.call` returns false and
`on_replies` moves on to the next op: nothing calls the fork's `connection_crashed`, which is what answers 500
and closes on every fork-owned path (F-5). The query pool's `on_done` (`workers.odin:192-195`) has the same shape.

**Scenario.** A pure trap while rendering a write's result (a bounds error in a view, a panic in a helper) after
`resume`. The connection is `.Active`, `res.sent` is false, its deferred watch was ended by `resume`, and no read is
in flight, so the sweep skips it and nothing ever closes it. The client waits for its own timeout (htmx's 60 s);
the server keeps the fd, the arena and the `conn_count` slot for the life of the process, and at shutdown the loop
waits the full `DRAIN_DEADLINE_MS` for it. The op (`defer store.free_op`) and `web.tl_ctx` (the skipped `finish`)
are left behind too. The guard's burst rule bounds the count (100 a minute), not the effect. D15 rule 3's "pure →
answer 500" is not met for this entry point.

**Fix.** Make the continuation an entry point the fork can finish: for example `web.resume_call(d, fn)`, which
resumes, runs `fn` through `guard.call`, and on a failed call after a successful resume calls a new fork proc
(`http.response_crashed(ref)`, which runs `connection_crashed` on the connection the ref names). Use it in the
writer's `on_replies` and the pools' `on_done`. Add a TEST_ROUTES drill that crashes inside a continuation.

### M1-05 · MEDIUM · shutdown: replies into a released loop, and an unbounded store close

**Where.** Each loop waits for the deferred responses it owes only until `drain_deadline`
(`server.odin:405`: `if !past_deadline { waiting += td.deferred }`), then waits at most 500 ms for leftovers and
releases its event loop (`server.odin:417-421`); `core:nbio`'s release zeroes the loop (`l^ = {}`). The writer's
intake stays open during the whole drain, and `main` closes the store only after every loop has returned
(`cmd/skeleton/main.odin:30-31`); `writer_stop` commits whatever is still queued and posts each reply with
`nbio.next_tick_poly(…, l = op.loop)` (`writer.odin:186-215`). `app.shutdown` stops the watchdog before
`web.stop` (`app.odin:242-247`), and `store_sqlite.close` joins the writer and the query pool with no deadline
(`open.odin:119-124`).

**Scenario.** SIGTERM while a batch waits on `SQLITE_BUSY` (busy_timeout 5000 ms: another process holding the
database's write lock, such as a `sqlite3` shell or a second instance during a deploy), or while thousands of
writes are queued on a slow disk. The loops give up after 5 s and release; the writer then posts into a zeroed
loop (or into freed thread-local memory, once the loop's thread has exited). Reading `core:nbio`: the op comes from
the destroyed pool, the enqueue into the zeroed queue fails, and `exec` retries with `wake_up`; on Linux
`_wake_up` dereferences the zeroed `l.wake` on the writer thread, outside any guard, so the process dies with
SIGSEGV before the log flush and the WAL checkpoint; on Windows the enqueue never succeeds and `exec` spins
(`warn`, `wake_up`, `_yield`), so `writer_stop`'s join never returns and the container is killed at the 10 s
grace. The exact faulting line is suspected; the class is confirmed: `workers.odin:111-115` records it from S0.5
("a reply posted after its loop has left would land in a dead event loop", which crashed in nbio's `wake_up`).
The pools are drained at the start of shutdown; the writer is not. The clean path has no hard deadline after the watchdog stops (§8.7 promises a total ≤ 6 s).
Data is safe (a batch is committed before its replies are posted); the exit is not.

**Fix.** Stop the writer's intake when the drain starts (answer new writes with `.Busy` → 503, as the pools do),
and never post to a loop that has left: for example a per-loop "accepting replies" flag the loop clears before
`release_thread_event_loop`, which `post_replies` checks (freeing the op instead of posting it). Arm a hard exit
for the clean path too (e.g. `DRAIN_DEADLINE_MS` + 1.5 s from the start of `app.shutdown`), as the dirty path has.

### M1-06 · MEDIUM · a crashed batch is answered as committed

**Where.** `writer_run` ignores `guard.call`'s result (`writer.odin:150`) and posts the batch's replies
(`writer.odin:153`). `write` set every op's `err` to `.None` when it was queued (`writer.odin:50`).

**Scenario.** A trap inside the k-th op's `run`. It is dirty (the write lock is held), so the process will drain
and exit 70, but first `post_replies` answers the whole batch: ops 1..k-1 have `.None` from their own run, ops k..n
never ran and still carry the `.None` from `write`. Their continuations answer 200 ("Contact added.", a row built
from a zero `result`: id 0, empty name), while the transaction never reaches `COMMIT` and is rolled back at the
next open. Clients are told their writes succeeded when none did. (The writer then deadlocks on its own lock at
the next batch; the hard exit and the watchdog end that.)

**Fix.** `if !guard.call(commit_batch, head) { fail_batch(head, "crashed") }` before `post_replies`, so every op
answers `.Failed` (500); or post nothing and let the drain answer.

### M1-07 · MEDIUM · a reader that can't be opened exits the process

**Where.** Readers open lazily, on each loop's first read (`reader` → `open_reader`, `sqlite.odin:187-211`).
`open_conn` calls the store's `fatal` on any failure (`sqlite.odin:120-125`), which is `os.exit(1)`: written for
boot, reachable from a request handler at runtime. F-8 reserves 64 descriptors below the soft limit
(`limits_posix.odin:14`).

**Scenario.** The connection cap is reached (each accepted connection is a descriptor) before some loop has read.
The headroom then has to hold the listener, stdio, the writer's database and WAL files, each loop's io_uring ring
and wake eventfd, and every reader's two files. With 16 loops that is about 39 descriptors before any reader plus
2 per reader, which passes 64 after roughly a dozen readers. The next first read on another loop fails with
EMFILE and the process exits 1: no drain, no crash file, every loop gone. Suspected arithmetic (it depends on
`THREADS` and on SQLite's descriptors per connection); confirm by filling the cap with idle connections on a
freshly started server, then sending reads until every loop has one. Behind the stack's proxy the cap (65,472 with
compose's 65536) is out of reach of one upstream address; a direct exposure or a host with a low hard limit is
not. Any other transient open failure (ENOMEM, a file replaced under the server) takes the same path.

**Fix.** Open each loop's reader in its F-4 init hook (the store can register one) so a missing descriptor fails
at boot, and at runtime return `.Failed` (500) from the read instead of exiting. Consider counting the per-loop
descriptors into F-8's reserve.

### M1-08 · LOW · odin-http's own log lines go nowhere

`obs.logger()` (`obs.odin:208`) exists but nothing installs it: no `context.logger =` appears anywhere under
`app/`, and Odin's default logger discards every line (`base:runtime` `default_logger_proc`: "Nothing"). The
skeleton installed a console logger first thing in `main`; the rewrite lost it. So every `log.*` call in the fork
is dropped: F-8's accept errors and "Connection limit reached", F-12's io_uring and seccomp explanation and the bind
error (a seccomp-blocked container exits with only `listen … failed: Unsupported_Socket` in `app.fatal`'s line),
"could not send response", request-scanner errors, and F-9's "cutting it" line. Rubric 5 asks for errors logged
server-side. Fix: `context.logger = obs.logger()` in `app.boot` right after `obs.init`, before any thread starts
(threads inherit the context); demote the fork's info-level `connection %i already closing/closed`
(`server.odin:527`) to debug, or it becomes noise.

### M1-09 · LOW · request identity is per thread where requests interleave

- `obs.next_request_id` returns a slice of the thread-local `tl_req.id` (`obs.odin:275-281`); `web.request_id`
  keeps it as `c.id` and as the `x-request-id` header value without copying (`web/front.odin:199-207`). A response
  written after another request started on the same loop (every deferred write's continuation; a handler reached
  after a body that arrived in a later packet) carries the other request's id, and `front`'s `done` logs it.
- `defer_response` takes its start time from the thread-local `c_start` (`web/front.odin:293,321-324`), not
  `c.start`, so a deferred write reached after an async body read logs another request's latency.
- `obs.set_request` runs only at the top of `front` (`app/front.odin:42`); an async body callback, `web.resume` and
  `web.finish` don't restore it, so a crash line or a watchdog stall there names the last request the loop started
  (D15 rule 8: "always log what died").

Fix: clone the generated id into the request arena; use `c.start`; call `obs.set_request` in `call` and in
`web.resume`.

### M1-10 · LOW · the drain's log lines are never flushed

`loop_stop` (F-4's shutdown hook) flushes the loop's buffer and disarms its 1 s tick (`app.odin:271-276`,
`crash.odin:103-110`), and the fork calls it before it waits for the connections (`server.odin:363`). Everything the
loop logs afterwards (access lines for requests finished during the drain, deferred continuations' lines, the
fork's `shutdown: done`) stays in the thread's buffer, which nothing flushes when the thread ends; `app.exit`
flushes only the main thread's. §8.7 step 7 says the per-thread buffers are flushed. Fix: a flush after the fork's
wait loop (an `on_thread_exit` hook next to F-4's two), or switch a stopping loop to write-through.

### M1-11 · LOW · half-closed clients and resets

- F-13's watch treats a readable socket that reads 0 bytes as the peer leaving (`deferred.odin:84-97`), so a client
  that sends its request and half-closes (`shutdown(SHUT_WR)`, `nc`, scripted tools) never gets a deferred write's
  response, though the write commits. A direct response is unaffected (the scanner isn't reading then).
- Past the connection cap, the 503 is sent and the socket closed at once with the request still unread
  (`server.odin:588-592`); the unread bytes make the close a reset, which can destroy the 503 before the client
  reads it. Refusals that leave a body unread (400 for a bad length, 413) reset 500 ms later.
- The drills' `raw()` resolves `'ERR'` on any socket error, even after the response arrived
  (`drills.mjs:283-293`), and `'ERR'` is tallied as "none". TODO slice 10 notes 5,560 "none" of 10,000 on Linux
  against 1,250 on Windows. Suspected: most of the difference is resets after a response, plus cap 503s if the
  test host's descriptor limit is low (the cap counts the 500 ms close linger). Confirm by recording `got` on error
  and the server's 503 count in that run.

Fix: on a 0-byte read while deferred, keep the response (only an error or `POLLHUP`/`RDHUP` with nothing to read
should mean gone; or ignore the watch once the full request was read); before closing a refused connection, read
and discard what is pending for a short while (lingering close); make `raw()` keep what it received.

### M1-12 · LOW · application callbacks outside the guard

`on_gone` runs from `_deferred_end` (`deferred.odin:107`), which `connection_close` calls; the fork's shutdown loop
calls `connection_close` unguarded (`server.odin:379,393`), and so does `connection_crashed` after a recovery
(`server.odin:659`). A stream's `on_shutdown` (`stream.odin:208`) and `on_close` (`stream.odin:329`) run from the
same unguarded paths. No M1 code sets `on_gone` or opens a stream, so this is latent until M2. Live today: the
writer's idle `ping` steps SQLite outside `guard.call` (`writer.odin:147,231-243`), and `post_replies` is unguarded;
a trap there dies with a signal, no crash line and no crash file. Fix: call application callbacks through `_invoke`
in the fork, and wrap `ping` in `guard.call` (it is dirty either way, but then it is logged and drained).

### M1-13 · LOW · the crash-zone contract is not enforced

PLAN §19.2.1 runs `tools/check locks` (no `sync` lock outside the guard helpers) and `entry-points` (every
`nbio.*_poly`, thread and timer callback wrapped or waived) on every push; `tools/check` implements imports,
profile, routes, contracts and parity only (`main.odin:31`). Plain `sync.Mutex` is used in the writer queue
(`writer.odin:33`), the pools and their registry (`workers.odin:41,77`), the scheduler (`jobs.odin:36`), obs
(`obs.odin:39`, `metrics.odin:46`, `health.odin:46`), the reader list (`store/sqlite/sqlite.odin:82`) and guard's
burst counter. `guard.posted()` is called only by the `async` drill (`web/drills.odin:68`), so `async_posted` is
always 0 in production. Today's posts are safe by construction (ops own their memory, refs are generation-checked,
the reader timer points at heap state), and the queue locks guard code that can't trap, but nothing stops M2's hub
or timers from posting a raw op or taking a plain lock around code that can. Fix: implement the two checks, with
reasoned waivers for the queue locks; state in `guard.odin` which post paths are crash-safe without `posted()`.

### M1-14 · LOW · Markdown's worst case is quadratic

`md_inline` calls `link_at` at every `[`, and `link_at` scans to the end of the line when no `]` closes it
(`markdown.odin:450-457,520-538`); `md_emphasis` searches for the closing marker from every `*`, `_` and `~`
(`markdown.odin:500-505`). A 64 KiB line of `[` (MAX_INPUT) is about 2×10⁹ byte steps: seconds of one loop
(suspected; confirm with a timing test). No M1 route renders Markdown, and the caps (input 64 KiB, depth 32) are in
place, so this is for the first caller (chat, wiki): bound the scans (stop `link_at` at the first `]` with depth 0,
or remember that no `]` follows), or render off the loop. There is no fuzz case for the parser (rubric 7).

### M1-15 · LOW · the watchdog's lock limit equals busy_timeout

`commit_batch` takes the writer's `guard.Mutex` and then runs `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` (`writer.odin:162-164`), which may
wait the connection's busy_timeout of 5000 ms (`sqlite.odin:129`) when another process holds the write lock. The
watchdog exits 70 when a watched lock is held for more than 5 s (`watchdog.odin:20`). A legitimate busy wait plus
the batch can cross it, depending on the 1 s phase of the watchdog's check. Only another process can cause the
wait (the app has one writer), so the exposure is operational. Fix: LOCK_LIMIT above busy_timeout plus a commit
(e.g. 10 s), or a shorter busy_timeout on the writer.

### M1-16 · LOW · unchecked setup results

`posix.sigaltstack` (`trap_posix.odin:63`) and `posix.sigaction` (`:48`) results are ignored: a thread without an
alternate stack can't run the handler on a stack overflow, and the process dies silently.
`AddVectoredExceptionHandler`'s nil result is ignored (`trap_windows.odin:40`): no guard at all, silently.
`store.new_op` ignores `mem.alloc_bytes`' error (`store.odin:88`) and dereferences the result. The form fallbacks
`role, _ := m.role_from(…)` and `status, _ := …` (contacts and gallery web) lack the one-line justification rubric
5 asks for. Fix: make the guard's setup failures fatal at boot; return nil from `new_op` and answer 503.

### Findings

**CRITICAL:** none confirmed by reading; M1-17 would rank CRITICAL if a run confirms it.

#### M1-17 · HIGH (suspected) · the chunked decoder recurses once per token

**Where.** `scanner.odin:184` calls the scan callback synchronously whenever the buffer already holds a token, and
each continuation of the chunked decoder re-enters `scanner_scan` as its last act (`body.odin:243`, `:277`, `:274`,
`:227`, `:310`). So the chunks one read delivers are parsed as a call chain about two frames deep per token, three
tokens per chunk, unwinding only when the buffer runs dry. Only the body cap bounds the run, and it counts data bytes
only: 65,536 one-byte chunks under `MAX_BODY`, 262,144 under the post-response drain's 256 KiB, which any GET with
`Transfer-Encoding: chunked` reaches.

**Scenario.** One write of the request headers, then 40,000 × `1\r\na\r\n`, then `0\r\n\r\n` (240 KB), to any write
path, an unknown path, or `GET /healthz`. Loop threads get `core:thread`'s default stack: the executable's 1 MiB
reserve on Windows, RLIMIT_STACK (typically 8 MiB) on Linux. If the stack overflows, the trap is recovered as pure,
answered 500 and counted toward GUARD_BURST: about 101 a minute exit 70, M1-01's chain exactly. Through Caddy it is
suspected unreachable (Go's proxy re-chunks the body in large writes). The header scan recurses the same way
(`server.odin:806`), bounded by `limit_headers`' 8,000 bytes.

**Fix.** Iterate instead of recursing: a nested `scanner_scan` call made from inside a callback records its
continuation and returns, and the outer call loops while tokens remain. Until then, a chunk-count cap bounds this and
M1-18. Add the payload to `MALFORMED`.

#### M1-18 · MEDIUM · chunk framing is uncounted, and the scanner never compacts within a request

The cap counts only chunk data (`body.odin:231`). A size line may be up to 64 KiB (`server.odin:822`,
`body.odin:254`); everything after `;` is dropped, and F-14 accepts any amount of trailing whitespace (`:218`). Within
a request the scanner only grows (`scanner.odin:200-228`). One-byte chunks each with a ~64 KiB extension, 65,536 of
them under a 64 KiB cap, hold about 4 GiB in one connection's buffer (16 GiB on the drain path), limited only by
`BODY_TIMEOUT_MS` times the client's bandwidth. Direct binds only; upstream behaviour F-14 kept. **Fix:** a small
`max_token_size` for size lines (4 KiB, Go's limit), framing bytes counted against the cap or a chunk-count cap, and
compaction before growing.

#### M1-19 · LOW · any scanner error ends the trailers as success

`body.odin:284` treats `err != nil` like the final empty line: a trailer line over 64 KiB, a reset, or the body
timeout after the last chunk all answer the body as complete. On `.Too_Long` the unread rest of the line is then
parsed as the next request, which can't form one (a framing defect, not smuggling). **Fix:** answer the error before
the empty-line test.

#### M1-20 · LOW · F-14's framing is looser than its UPSTREAM row says

`5  \r\n` (trailing whitespace, no extension) is accepted (`body.odin:218`). Size lines and the post-data terminator
are read with `bufio.scan_lines`, which drops one optional CR, so a bare LF passes the "CRLF" check (`:268`). A legal
size with leading zeros past 15 digits is refused. Not exploitable through a proxy that re-frames the body; a proxy
that forwards framing verbatim and parses strictly could disagree on bare LF. **Fix:** CRLF only in chunk framing,
whitespace only before `;`, leading zeros skipped before counting digits.

#### M1-21 · LOW · the 15-digit bound assumes a 64-bit int

F-14 dropped upstream's `#assert(size_of(i64) == size_of(int))`; on a 32-bit target `parse_chunk_size` wraps at 8
digits and `scan_num_bytes` asserts `n >= 0` (`scanner.odin:22`). No supported target is 32-bit. **Fix:**
`#assert(size_of(int) == 8)`.

#### M1-22 · LOW · M1-08 made client-caused failures log at warn and error

With `obs.logger()` installed, the fork's lines for a malformed request are live at warn (`server.odin:715, 738, 752,
778, 790, 799, 813`), one per connection for any client, at the level `LOG_LEVEL=warn` keeps. Two print request bytes,
and `obs.str` escapes only `"`, `\`, LF, CR and TAB, so other control bytes, ESC sequences included, reach a terminal
tailing the log. A client that resets before its response is sent logs at error (`response.odin:360`). **Fix:**
client-caused parse and send failures at info or debug; the other C0 bytes and DEL as `\xNN`.

#### M1-23 · LOW · the tests miss what the changes most need

F-14's parser has no unit test or fuzz case (rubric 7); only the drills' payloads cover it. The router test calls
`router_find` while `context.allocator` is a throwaway arena, so it can't catch a per-request allocation on
`context.allocator`. No case pins mount order within a method, or the two-segment bucket winning over the one-segment
one. **Fix:** restore the runner's allocator around the lookups; add `GET /contacts/(%d+)` before
`GET /contacts/(.+)` and `GET /ui/(.+)` before `GET /ui/tab`.
